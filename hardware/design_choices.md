# Design Decisions

## Objective
Designed as a compact, battery-powered PWM motor controller for small brushed drone motors,
with an emphasis on efficiency, wireless control, and future scalability.

## ESP32
Chosen for its integrated Wi-Fi, enabling a custom browser-based dashboard without additional communication hardware.
Its hardware PWM peripherals provide reliable motor control, while the large number of GPIO pins allows future expansion for
sensors, displays, and additional peripherals.

## Initial Prototype

### IRLB8748 MOSFET
Selected for its low RDS(on) and high current capability, providing efficient PWM switching with minimal conduction losses during prototyping.

### Schottky Flyback Diode
Added to suppress inductive voltage spikes generated by the motors, protecting the switching circuitry and improving overall reliability.

### Decoupling Capacitors
A 470 µF capacitor stabilizes the motor supply during rapid load changes,
while 0.1 µF ceramic capacitors filter high-frequency switching noise for stable operation  near the power sources and motors.
Capacitors placed as close as possible to the noise sources to effectively smoothen signals. 

## Final Design Improvements

### AO3400A MOSFET
Replaced the IRLB8748 (THT-Package) with the AO3400A (SMD-Package) to significantly reduce PCB size while maintaining efficient switching performance,
making the design better suited for compact, battery-powered applications.

### TPS63031 Buck-Boost Converter
Added to provide a regulated supply from a single-cell 3.7V Li-ion battery to the ESP32 and the motors. 
The TPS63031 boosts the 3.7V to 5V for the ESP, while the motors still run on the 3.7V directly from the battery. 
Eliminates the need to power the ESP with USB and make the board independent, once code is uploaded to the ESP.
